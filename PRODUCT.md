# Product

## Who uses it, and when

A team in a video call needs one person for a job nobody wants: taking
notes, running the retro, writing the release notes. One person opens
LuckyYou and shares their screen, everyone watches a short, silly show, and
the app names who gets the job. The app's text is in English.

## The joke

Winning is losing. The person picked is congratulated as if they had won a
prize. The humour is light and work-safe.

## Readable over a screen share

Everyone else sees the app through a video call, compressed and scaled down.
Text is big, contrast is strong, and nothing important is small, faint or too
quick to follow.

## Non-goals

- No accounts, server or database.
- No history or stats: every pick stands alone.
- No in-app scenario editor.
- Made for one team, not a public product.

## Anti-goals

These would be failures even if everything else worked:

- A pick that isn't truly random, or a scenario that can influence it.
- A new scenario that needs edits outside its own folder.
- Text that can't be read over a screen share.

## Anti-references

LuckyYou must never look or feel like:

- the stopwatch race sites: clutter, upsell buttons and the same race every
  time;
- a classroom site: clip-art and rainbows everywhere;
- a casino: flashing lights or anything that hints at gambling;
- a corporate form: grey boxes and small text;
- anything mean or gory: nobody gets hurt, and the joke is on the job, not
  the person.
